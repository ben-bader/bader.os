# Creative direction

## North star

Software should feel tangible and open to exploration. The portfolio uses a dark, spatial editorial language: midnight ink surfaces, mist colored type, precise rules, a controlled electric lime signal, and one procedural glass loop in a shared WebGL scene. The loop suggests connected layers in a system; it is an atmospheric object, not a logo or a diagram of a specific project.

The page makes Bader's identity and selected work easy to find first, then gives visitors a clear route through background, demonstrated tools, education, internships, and contact. Project maps use nodes and edges from `lib/project-architecture.ts`, keyed to canonical project records. They communicate documented relationships and do not stand in for product screenshots.

## Visitor sequence

1. Understand who Bader is and what he builds.
2. Explore AtlasHR and ONEE-SYS, including project context, contribution, status, and architecture.
3. Scan the two additional project records.
4. Read background, project-supported capabilities, and real chronology.
5. Contact Bader or open a verified professional link.

## Rules

- Preserve the existing project and career facts. Do not infer proficiency from a technology list.
- Keep project, education, and internship content in semantic server rendered HTML.
- Use one persistent R3F canvas for the interactive glass loop and project map focus. The canvas is decorative and nonessential content remains in the DOM.
- Keep the accent selective. It marks actions, section position, and material edges rather than filling the page.
- Keep navigation and project details operable with keyboard, touch, and reduced motion.
- Do not add fake product screenshots, metrics, statuses, or external project URLs.
