# Frontend Laws

## Philosophy

These are immutable design laws.

They are not suggestions.

Every component, page and interaction must obey these rules.

Whenever two rules conflict, prioritize:

User Experience > Accessibility > Performance > Visual Effects.

---

# Law 1

Design for humans before developers.

---

# Law 2

Whitespace is a component.

Never treat empty space as wasted space.

---

# Law 3

The eye should always know where to look first.

Every section must have one dominant focal point.

---

# Law 4

Never allow two primary visual elements to compete.

One hero.

Everything else supports it.

---

# Law 5

Typography creates hierarchy.

Do not rely only on color.

---

# Law 6

Never center everything.

Use alignment intentionally.

---

# Law 7

Every section must breathe.

Avoid crowded layouts.

---

# Law 8

Consistency creates trust.

Spacing.

Radius.

Shadows.

Typography.

Animations.

Everything must follow one system.

---

# Law 9

Never invent spacing values.

Use the spacing scale only.

4

8

12

16

24

32

40

48

64

80

96

128

---

# Law 10

Margins separate siblings.

Padding protects children.

Never confuse the two.

---

# Law 11

Content determines layout.

Never force layouts that fight the content.

---

# Law 12

Large typography should feel confident.

Never oversized just to impress.

---

# Law 13

Every animation must explain something.

Never animate for decoration.

---

# Law 14

If removing an animation improves usability,

remove the animation.

---

# Law 15

Motion should reinforce hierarchy.

Important elements animate first.

---

# Law 16

Avoid repetitive fade-ins.

Use different reveal techniques.

Mask.

Scale.

Depth.

Clip-path.

Perspective.

Parallax.

---

# Law 17

Objects should feel physical.

Weight.

Momentum.

Inertia.

Anticipation.

Follow-through.

---

# Law 18

Buttons should invite interaction.

Not demand attention.

---

# Law 19

Hover states should confirm intention.

Not surprise users.

---

# Law 20

Scrolling is storytelling.

Never a list of sections.

---

# Law 21

Every section should have a different rhythm.

Avoid repeating animation patterns.

---

# Law 22

Three.js exists to support storytelling.

Not to show technical skill.

---

# Law 23

3D should never hide content.

---

# Law 24

Lighting is more important than geometry.

---

# Law 25

Backgrounds should support foreground.

Never compete.

---

# Law 26

Depth should feel subtle.

Avoid exaggerated perspective.

---

# Law 27

Glass effects require restraint.

Too much glass destroys hierarchy.

---

# Law 28

Use shadows sparingly.

Prefer depth through spacing.

---

# Law 29

Borders should separate.

Not decorate.

---

# Law 30

Rounded corners should follow one system.

Never random radii.

---

# Law 31

Every image deserves a reveal animation.

---

# Law 32

Every headline deserves an entrance.

---

# Law 33

Paragraphs should never animate excessively.

Readability comes first.

---

# Law 34

Never animate layout shifts.

---

# Law 35

Navigation should disappear until needed.

---

# Law 36

The cursor should enhance.

Never distract.

---

# Law 37

Scrolling should feel effortless.

Use Lenis.

Avoid browser default.

---

# Law 38

Pinned sections should have purpose.

Not every section deserves pinning.

---

# Law 39

Horizontal scrolling should tell stories.

Never replace normal navigation.

---

# Law 40

Project showcases deserve the longest interaction time.

---

# Law 41

Case studies are experiences.

Not cards.

---

# Law 42

Never show GitHub repositories before featured work.

---

# Law 43

Featured projects are handcrafted.

GitHub projects are automated.

Separate them.

---

# Law 44

Every interaction should feel handcrafted.

Never generic.

---

# Law 45

Icons support text.

They never replace meaning.

---

# Law 46

Accessibility is not optional.

Keyboard.

ARIA.

Reduced Motion.

Contrast.

Semantic HTML.

Always.

---

# Law 47

Performance is a feature.

Never sacrifice FPS for decoration.

---

# Law 48

Optimize before adding features.

---

# Law 49

Code quality affects UI quality.

Bad architecture produces inconsistent interfaces.

---

# Law 50

Reusable components create consistent experiences.

---

# Law 51

Never duplicate animation logic.

---

# Law 52

Never hardcode colors.

Use design tokens.

---

# Law 53

Never hardcode spacing.

Use variables.

---

# Law 54

Never hardcode transitions.

Use animation presets.

---

# Law 55

Never create one-off components.

Generalize.

---

# Law 56

Every section should have an emotional goal.

Wonder.

Trust.

Curiosity.

Confidence.

Excitement.

Inspiration.

---

# Law 57

Every page should have visual rhythm.

Fast.

Slow.

Pause.

Reveal.

Repeat.

---

# Law 58

Whitespace creates luxury.

Clutter creates anxiety.

---

# Law 59

Large layouts require restraint.

Minimalism is harder than decoration.

---

# Law 60

Less components.

Better components.

---

# Law 61

Every scroll position should feel composed.

Never accidental.

---

# Law 62

Never use placeholders in production.

---

# Law 63

Animations should respect reduced-motion preferences.

---

# Law 64

Every hover state should complete within 200–300ms.

---

# Law 65

Every page should have one memorable moment.

A hero interaction.

A cinematic transition.

A unique 3D scene.

A surprising microinteraction.

Give users something they'll remember.

---

# Law 66

Never rely on color alone to convey information.

---

# Law 67

Hierarchy is created by contrast:

- size
- spacing
- weight
- position
- motion

Use color last.

---

# Law 68

Responsiveness is redesign.

Not resizing.

Layouts should adapt thoughtfully across mobile, tablet, desktop, ultrawide, and high-DPI displays.

---

# Law 69

Every design decision should support the story of the portfolio.

If an effect exists only because it looks cool, remove it.

---

# Law 70

Build experiences, not pages.

The visitor should feel like they're exploring a carefully crafted digital product rather than scrolling through a résumé.

---

# Final Law

Whenever generating or refactoring UI, ask yourself:

"Would this feel at home on a premium product website from Apple, Linear, Vercel, or an award-winning creative studio?"

If the answer is no, iterate until the answer is yes.

Never stop at "good enough."

Pursue clarity, elegance, accessibility, maintainability, and craftsmanship in equal measure.
